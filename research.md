# Research and sources

## Earlier map projects

Several projects already tell or map the march of the Ten Thousand online. This site follows selected episodes from all seven books, including the service in Thrace and the handover to the Spartan commander Thibron at Pergamum.

- **Ages of the World** has a six-stage scroll-driven map in its chapter on fourth-century Greece. Its mapped account runs from Sardis through Cunaxa and Trapezus to Byzantium. [The March of the Ten Thousand](https://agesofeurope.eu/chapter-09-xenophon)
- **Tracking the Ten Thousand**, by Cian Colgan, models Xenophon's geography using GIS and text annotation. The project provides an interactive map, a text-and-map prototype and a downloadable research dataset. [ArcGIS StoryMap](https://storymaps.arcgis.com/stories/6dc40d9bcbb642d381913910d6568ce2) · [Reader prototype](https://ciancolgan.com/ten/thousand.html) · [Research repository](https://github.com/CianColgan/Tracking-the-Ten-Thousand)
- **Iordanis Paradeisopoulos's Greek-language companion** includes all seven books, essays on the route and chronology, and an illustrated map appendix. [Companion](https://xenophon.paradeisos.org/) · [Map appendix](https://xenophon.paradeisos.org/map/)
- **Teresita Cano Ricardez's 2015 thesis** examines the geography of each book and includes a route map crediting Emmaline Montserrat Rosado González on page 30. [UNAM thesis record](https://repositorio.unam.mx/contenidos/ficha/informacion-geografica-en-la-anabasis-de-jenofonte-291307) · [Full thesis](https://tesiunamdocumentos.dgb.unam.mx/ptd2015/junio/0730176/0730176.pdf)

## Read Xenophon

- **H. G. Dakyns's English translation** includes all seven books and is in the public domain in the United States. [Read the translation at Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1170/1170-h/1170-h.htm)
- **The Landmark Xenophon's Anabasis**, edited by Shane Brennan and David Thomas, includes a modern translation, maps, notes and an appendix on the route. [Pantheon edition](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/213098/the-landmark-xenophons-anabasis-by-shane-brennan-and-david-thomas-editors--robert-b-strassler-series-editor/)
- **Rüdiger Schmitt's “Anabasis”** summarizes all seven books and places the work in its Persian and Greek contexts. [Encyclopaedia Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/anabasis/)

## Geography

The route is schematic. Xenophon's sequence provides the itinerary, while the precise paths between many stops and the locations of places such as Thapsacus, Cunaxa and Mount Theches remain disputed.

- **Pleiades** offers entries and bibliographies for ancient places and their proposed identifications. [Pleiades Gazetteer of Ancient Places](https://pleiades.stoa.org/)
- **Shane Brennan and Christopher Tuplin** review several plausible locations for Mount Theches and explain how each depends on the route assumed. [“Theches: an elusive mountain”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/anatolian-studies/article/theches-an-elusive-mountain/CA88FCA84D4592918DA546745F8AD9D1)
- **Iordanis Paradeisopoulos** compares competing reconstructions and discusses the difficulties in reading Xenophon's reported distances. [“Route and Parasangs in Xenophon's Anabasis”](https://www.academia.edu/40044835/Route_and_Parasangs_in_Xenophon_s_Anabasis)
- **Natural Earth** provides public-domain geographical data for present-day landforms and waterways. [Physical data](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/) · [Terms of use](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/)
