# Political geography around 401 BCE

The political layer shows approximate territories around the outset of Xenophon’s expedition. Its outlines are fixed across the story, which follows events into 399 BCE. Coastlines and terrain use modern physical geography. Ancient authority was often uneven, exercised through local rulers, and contested in the places the army crossed.

## Polity outlines

The outlines adapt Cliopatria v0.2.2 by James S. Bennett, Erin Mutch, Andrew Tollefson, Ed Chalstrey and colleagues for the Seshat Global History Databank. The selected records include 401 BCE in their date ranges. Coordinates were simplified with a 0.075° tolerance and rounded to three decimal places. Fragments smaller than 0.003 square degrees were omitted. [Dataset release](https://github.com/Seshat-Global-History-Databank/cliopatria/releases/tag/v0.2.2) · [Methods paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-025-04516-9) · [Dataset license, CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

The Cliopatria frame covering 401 BCE shades the whole of Egypt as Persian. Around 401 BCE Amyrtaeus already held power in the Delta, while documents still recognized the Persian king at Elephantine. To leave the transition area unshaded, the later Egyptian footprint from Cliopatria’s 383–367 BCE frame was subtracted from the imperial polygon as a mask. Four small fragments near Suez and the eastern Delta were removed as artifacts of the subtraction. The resulting edge marks the margin of the shading in this atlas. Egypt has a context pin. [Cambridge Ancient History on Egypt, 404–332 BCE](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-ancient-history/egypt-404332-bc/7B70F72C3389EB2B29D646F7BAA6318D)

Athens and Sparta are shown as individual city-states. The Achaemenid outline includes Cyprus to indicate the island’s general position in the imperial sphere, with a marker at Salamis for the local authority of Evagoras. [The Greek City States](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805218/50490/excerpt/9780521850490_excerpt.pdf) · [Antigoni Zournatzi on Cyprus](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/cyprus-achaemenid/)

## Administrative geography

Administrative points mark known or proposed seats, including Sardis, Dascylium, Celaenae, Tarsus, Babylon, and Damascus. The Cappadocia and Armenian points indicate broad regions. Bruno Jacobs’s survey draws on royal inscriptions and Greek and Roman sources, which describe different periods and levels of government. Xenophon names Orontas in Armenia, Tiribazus in Western Armenia, and the local ruler Syennesis at Tarsus. Provincial polygons are omitted because the available evidence does not support a uniform set of boundaries for 401 BCE. [Bruno Jacobs, *Achaemenid Satrapies*](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/achaemenid-satrapies/) · [Xenophon, *Anabasis* 1.2](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Anabasis_(Dakyns)/Book_1/Chapter_2) · [3.5](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Anabasis_(Dakyns)/Book_3/Chapter_5) · [4.4](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Anabasis_(Dakyns)/Book_4/Chapter_4) · [7.2](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Anabasis_(Dakyns)/Book_7/Chapter_2)

Rounded site coordinates locate the ancient places at map scale. For example, the Dascylium point is at the excavated mound near Ergili, and Ecbatana is shown in modern Hamadan. These values do not increase the precision of a historical jurisdiction. [Dascylium in ToposText](https://topostext.org/place/401281PDas) · [Ecbatana in the UNESCO World Heritage List](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1716)


## World coverage around 401 BCE

The world map adds 12 reviewed outlines to the three expedition polities. It also marks 27 political centers, archaeological sites, and communities. **World view** rotates to seven geographic regions, and the world index links each entry to its source.

The outlines use Cliopatria v0.2.2 records whose inclusive date range covers 401 BCE. The generation script filters composite duplicates, generalizes geometry, and preserves closed rings and holes. The displayed selection excludes records with strong chronological or geographic conflicts. The catalogue links interpretive sources as well as the dataset attribution. [Bennett et al., *Scientific Data*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-025-04516-9) · [Cliopatria v0.2.2](https://github.com/Seshat-Global-History-Databank/cliopatria/releases/tag/v0.2.2) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

- Early Rome and independent Veii are marked as centers. The source outline includes Veii before the traditional date of its capture and misses Rome’s site. [Livy’s chronology](https://www.livius.org/sources/content/livy/livy-periochae-1-5/)
- Eastern Zhou, Qin, Wei, Han, and Yan use points where the source boundaries miss the documented or proposed political centers. Magadha uses a point at Rajgir because its source outline generalizes a much broader extent than the evidence supports at this date. [Asia records](world-context-asia.mjs)
- At early Monte Albán, the strongest evidence for territorial state expansion comes from later phases. Other sites across the Americas and Oceania use symbols for archaeological evidence and communities. Unshaded land can contain inhabited territories and substantial societies. [Monte Albán research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2867764/) · [Site records](world-sites.mjs)
