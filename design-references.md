# Design references

Five references for the globe, timeline, and illustrated route in Anabasis Visualizer. The design ideas describe patterns to carry forward as the site develops.

## Ancient Earth

The linked view opens at 240 million years ago. A single globe stays in view as readers drag the timeline, step with arrow keys, or jump to a different era. Display options and credits are available alongside the globe. [Ancient Earth at 240 million years ago](https://dinosaurpictures.org/ancient-earth#240)

Use one continuous globe across all 15 scenes. Treat the timeline stops as narrative milestones, each with a place name and a brief context caption. Keep map display settings out of the main story controls.

## Earth Nullschool

The linked view selects surface winds and an orthographic camera. A show-and-hide menu holds dates, overlays, projections, and their data descriptions. The link preserves the selected projection and camera position. [Earth Nullschool orthographic view](https://earth.nullschool.net/#current/wind/surface/level/orthographic=-65.18,2.22,388) · [Controls and data notes](https://earth.nullschool.net/about.html)

Keep the globe clear, with zoom and route reset at the edge. Use motion to show a camera move or the progress of the current leg, and give readers a stable link to return to a scene.

## The Guardian’s *Bussed Out*

The story opens with an individual trip and then widens to show the pattern across many journeys. Scroll-driven graphics reveal movement in sequence alongside reporting, photos and video. A methodology note explains how the locations were matched and which records were omitted. [Bussed Out](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/ng-interactive/2017/dec/20/bussed-out-america-moves-homeless-people-country-study) · [Nadieh Bremer on making the journey map](https://www.nexerdigital.com/camp-digital/archive/2023/nadieh-bremer/)

Let readers follow one leg at a time. Give the current leg more visual weight while leaving the earlier route visible for orientation. Pair each scene with a single strong image, a brief account of what happened, and a way to check Xenophon’s passage and the basis for its plotted location.

## Ostellus Atlas

The atlas ties a large pannable map to a bottom timeline with zoomable time intervals. Events open more detail on demand, and links can specify the map center and zoom. [Ostellus Atlas reference view](https://atlas.ostellus.com/en/?zoom=3&lat=34.09640095616637&lon=52.86631843590226) · [Documented map and timeline examples](https://kartdok.staatsbibliothek-berlin.de/servlets/MCRFileNodeServlet/kartdok_derivate_00000668/Evaluating-Spatial-Visualisations-for-DigiKAR.pdf#page=102)

Keep the route visible when the timeline changes. Offer a consistent route overview so readers can return from a close-up or a freely rotated globe without losing their place in the story.

## Chronas

Chronas pairs a map with a fixed bottom timeline, layer controls, and a right-hand panel for selected places. Its links can restore a viewport and a selected item. [Chronas](https://chronas.org/) · [Chronas frontend](https://github.com/Chronasorg/chronas-frontend)

Make timeline, route phases, and pins select the same scene. Place the illustration and passage details at the side on wide screens and below the globe on small screens, so readers keep their orientation while exploring the evidence.
